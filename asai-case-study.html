Helping AI search understand a local education institute

A project focused on improving how AI tools discover and describe Atmasamman School of AI (ASAI) when people ask questions about AI learning options in Bulandshahr.

1. About ASAI

Atmasamman School of AI (ASAI) is an education and career-development initiative focused on practical technology skills. Its learning areas include AI and related technical subjects such as Python, cloud, DevOps and SQL. Its purpose is to help learners build skills they can apply in their education and careers.

ASAI operates under Atmasamman Foundation, alongside AIMT (Atmasamman Institute of Management and Technology). ASAI and AIMT are part of the Foundation’s wider education and skills-development work.

Organization background is summarized for context. For the most current official description, refer to ASAI and Atmasamman’s own public materials.

2. The problem

People do not always find information through a traditional search-results page. They may ask AI assistants questions such as “Where can I learn AI in Bulandshahr?” or “Which local institute offers practical AI training?”

For a local institute, being useful in these answers depends on whether public information is easy to discover, understand and connect to the question being asked. If programmes, location, audience or learning outcomes are unclear, an AI-generated response may omit the institute or describe it incompletely.

The project therefore focused on a practical question: How can ASAI’s public website communicate its programmes and local relevance more clearly, so AI tools have better information to work with?

3. My approach

Understand the local intent

I focused the work on questions about AI education and institutes in Bulandshahr. The goal was to examine the kind of information a person might need when exploring local learning options.

Review the public website

I looked at how the website presents the organization, its programmes and supporting information. The review considered whether visitors and AI systems could understand what ASAI offers and why the information was relevant to a local learner.

Turn findings into recommendations

I translated the review into recommendations for clearer programme information, website structure, supporting content and local relevance. I also supported website and content improvements as part of the project.

Review AI-generated responses

I examined responses to the targeted local questions after the work and considered how ASAI was represented. The focus was not simply whether the name appeared, but whether the output was relevant to the question.

4. What the project involved

  • Framing a local AI-discovery problem rather than treating GEO as a generic SEO checklist.
  • Reviewing whether key information about ASAI and its programmes was clear and accessible.
  • Preparing practical website and content recommendations.
  • Supporting improvements intended to make the organization easier to understand online.
  • Checking relevant AI-generated responses and documenting observations.

5. Outcome

ASAI appeared more prominently in the AI-generated responses reviewed for the targeted local questions.The observed results suggested that clearer public information and local relevance can help an organization be represented more usefully in AI-assisted discovery.

This is a qualitative summary of the project’s observed outcome. It is not a claim that ASAI will appear in every answer, that all AI platforms behave the same way, or that the result will remain unchanged over time.

6. What I learned

  • Clarity helps both people and systems. Useful information should be understandable to a prospective learner first; AI discoverability is not a substitute for a good website.
  • Local intent needs its own focus. A national-level description may not answer a person’s question about options in one town.
  • Recommendations should connect to a problem. Website changes are more useful when each one addresses a clear information gap.
  • Results need context. AI responses can vary by wording, platform and time, so observations should be described with their limits.

7. Limitations and confidentiality

AI-generated responses can change over time and differ between tools. This project should be understood as a targeted local investigation, not a universal ranking study or a guarantee of future visibility.

To respect project confidentiality, this public case study does not include the full assessment matrix, scoring logic, exact query set, competitor-by-competitor analysis or detailed methodology. I can discuss the reasoning and decision process in an interview without publishing those internal details.

In summary

This project gave me practical experience connecting local user intent, website communication and AI-generated discovery. It also reinforced a product-management habit I value: explain the problem, make recommendations that can be acted on, and report outcomes without overstating the evidence.
