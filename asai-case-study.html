Evaluating ASAI's visibility in AI-generated recommendations

A practical GEO research project examining how ASAI communicates its programmes, supports learner decisions, and appears in AI-generated recommendations for education and training queries.

Organization Atmasamman School of AI
Location focus Bulandshahr, India
My contribution Research, audit & recommendations
Read full GEO PRD on Notion ↗

1. About ASAI

Atmasamman School of AI (ASAI) is an education and career-development initiative focused on practical technology skills. Its learning areas include Artificial Intelligence, Python, Data Analytics, and related technical subjects.

This project applied Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) research to examine how ASAI's public website communicates programme information and how the institute is represented in AI-generated answers to learner-oriented questions.

This case study presents selected research findings. Detailed assessment procedures and confidential methodology are intentionally excluded.

2. The problem

Prospective learners may ask AI assistants questions such as "Where can I learn AI near me?" or "Which institute offers practical, job-oriented technology training?"

For an education institute, being represented usefully in these responses depends partly on whether its public information communicates its programmes, audience, location, supporting evidence, and learner benefits clearly.

The project investigated two related questions:

  • How effectively does ASAI's website present programme information and support learner decisions?
  • How do AI assistants represent ASAI when responding to different education and training queries?

The objective was to identify strengths and actionable opportunities without assuming that website quality alone determines AI recommendations.

3. My approach

Understand learner intent

I examined discovery prompts involving training options, practical learning requirements, location, travel distance, budget, educational background, and career goals. This framed the research around realistic learner decisions rather than generic website visibility.

Audit programme-page GEO readiness

I assessed ASAI's programme pages using a structured diagnostic framework. The review covered programme structure, clarity, completeness, topic relevance, supporting evidence, freshness signals, and usefulness for learner decisions.

Benchmark website communication

I compared the audit findings against an anonymised benchmark to understand where ASAI performed strongly and where further supporting evidence or clearer information could improve the overall presentation.

Analyse AI-generated recommendations

I reviewed AI-generated responses to relevant education queries and documented whether ASAI appeared in recommendations and the context in which it was mentioned. The analysis considered differences across learner needs, locations, budgets, and query wording.

Identify improvement opportunities

I translated the findings into recommendations focused on programme communication, supporting evidence, career-outcome information, content freshness, and the usefulness of the website for prospective learners.

4. Programme GEO audit results

The audit assessed seven programme-page dimensions. ASAI showed strong results in relevance, structure, and learner decision support. Supporting evidence emerged as the clearest improvement opportunity.

5. Key findings

Strengths identified

  • Strong relevance: Programme content was closely aligned with its intended learning purpose.
  • Clear information: Programme pages communicated important learning details in an understandable format.
  • Decision support: Programme details, pricing information, and available recommendation tools supported learner evaluation.
  • Career-oriented information: The website communicated job-readiness support and career-development opportunities.
  • Distinct learner offerings: Internship-related information and institutional offerings addressed additional learner needs.

Improvement opportunities

  • Evidence and credibility: Strengthen programme claims with verifiable supporting material where available.
  • Freshness: Make relevant updates and content-maintenance signals more visible.
  • Career outcomes: Support job-readiness claims with appropriate evidence and authentic examples.
  • Visual proof: The audited gallery existed but had no media, presenting an opportunity to add authentic, permissioned material.
  • Measurement: Maintain consistent prompts and records to evaluate changes over time.

6. AI discovery observations

The discovery research covered questions about local education providers, practical AI training, career-oriented learning, and options suited to different budgets and geographic constraints.

ASAI appeared in some relevant AI-generated recommendation contexts. The prompts also showed that recommendations could vary when location, travel distance, budget, or learner requirements changed.

These observations helped document how ASAI was represented in the responses reviewed and provided context for further investigation of its AI discovery.

Measurement distinction

Website audit scores and AI recommendation observations are separate forms of evidence. The available records do not establish a quantified before-and-after increase in visibility attributable to the project.

7. Recommendations

  • Strengthen supporting evidence for programme and career-related claims.
  • Improve visible content updates and maintenance signals.
  • Add authentic supporting material where appropriate and available.
  • Preserve the strong relevance, structure, clarity, and decision-support information identified in the audit.
  • Reassess relevant AI-generated responses using a consistent prompt set.
  • Record future observations systematically to support reliable comparisons over time.

8. Outcome

An evidence-based assessment of ASAI's programme GEO readiness and AI discovery

The project documented a good overall programme GEO assessment, identified strengths in relevance and learner decision support, and highlighted evidence and proof as a key improvement area.

The discovery analysis also recorded ASAI in some relevant AI-generated recommendations. Together, these findings provided a structured view of website communication and AI representation for the learner queries examined.

No quantified visibility increase is claimed because the available records do not establish a consistent before-and- after baseline or prove that the work caused a particular recommendation outcome.

9. What I learned

  • Website quality and AI discovery are related but distinct. Strong programme content does not automatically establish how an AI assistant will respond.
  • Learner intent matters. Location, budget, educational background, and career goals can change the context of a recommendation.
  • Evidence matters. Clear programme descriptions are stronger when relevant claims are supported by credible proof.
  • Recommendations should address real gaps. Research is most useful when findings lead to specific, actionable improvements.
  • Measurement requires discipline. Consistent prompts and documented baselines are needed to assess changes responsibly.

10. Limitations and confidentiality

AI-generated responses can vary by system, prompt wording, context, and time. The observations represent the supplied research records and should not be interpreted as a universal ranking or a guarantee of future visibility.

This public case study excludes competitor identities, exact numerical dimension scores, the full diagnostic rubric, detailed scoring logic, the exact query set, and the operational process used to select and apply optimisation techniques.

The published findings are limited to the audit categories and discovery observations. Further measurement would require consistent repeated assessments and a documented comparison baseline.

In summary

This project applied independent Generative Engine Optimization research to a real-world education website. It combined programme-page auditing, AI discovery prompt analysis, comparative benchmarking, and actionable recommendations.

The work demonstrates a product-management approach to an emerging discovery problem: understand learner needs, assess available evidence, identify meaningful gaps, and define practical improvements without overstating the results.
